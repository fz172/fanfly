## TLDR

- Calibrated all control surfaces:
  - flap, aileron, trim, rudder, elevator
- Routed the parachute pull cable along the center channel
- Rerouted pitot/AoA/static pneumatic lines behind the dashboard for better organization

## Details

I spent most of the time performing calibration tasks today. I had to turn the avionics on and off quite a few times, so I disconnected the fuel boost pump power to avoid dry-firing them. Then I started the calibration tasks.

### Flap Calibration

I loosened the jamnuts on both flaps so the torque tubes could rotate and move the flaps up and down. Then I put the G3X in configuration mode and ran through the flap calibration process.

On my G3X, the calibration is at: Engine & Airframe -> GP2 Flap -> Calibrate.

I followed the calibration video from Midwest Panel Builders for both the flap and trim calibration. For the flaps, I raised them all the way up and bumped them down a little, then recorded the 0, 10, 20, and 30 degree positions. Lastly, I went to the Flap configuration page and set the partial flap extend speed to 90. Easy. I tested the flap full travel range a few times and let it sit at the 0-degree position, then confirmed the torque tube was in the right position and locked the jamnuts again.

### Trim Calibration

Then I moved on to trim calibration (Engine & Airframe -> GP1 Trim -> Calibrate). The three positions required during calibration were full up, full down, and neutral.

Full up/down was easy. To find neutral, I stuck a digital angle measure on the trim tab and set it to 0 when it was parallel to the top skin of the horizontal stabilizer. Then as I moved the trim tab up and down, the digital angle moved with it. I just moved the trim slowly until the readout showed 0.

### Rudder Calibration

To calibrate the rudder, I needed to ensure it moves 22 +/- 2 degrees on both sides. To measure the movement, I downloaded an app called Protractor from the Play Store and stuck my phone to the side of the rudder. Then as I moved the rudder, the app read a degree offset from the starting point. I checked the offset was 20.x degrees on both sides, so it was within range.

I think I can get the range closer to 22 degrees. I will leave it like this for now and adjust if the turning doesn’t feel right after I get the engine started.

### Routing the Pneumatic Lines

When I connected the pneumatic lines for the pitot/static system, I didn't have a good big picture of how everything should be routed. So the routing was not great.

The other day when I debugged the fuel boost pump, the pneumatic lines’ routing really bothered me, so I decided to redo them before the panel was fully closed.

I bought some more quick-push connectors and 1/4" lines in different colors. I cut them to size and rerouted them so the lines can be tied to structure instead of flying around behind the dash.
